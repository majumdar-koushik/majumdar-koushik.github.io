A simpler approach to task management

Too Many Tasks? Focus on What Needs Attention Now

A simpler approach to managing an overwhelming task list.

Having too many tasks is not always the problem. The real problem is having to look at all of them when only a few actually need your attention right now.

Your task list may contain work, personal commitments, long-term goals, recurring activities and things you simply cannot afford to forget.

When everything appears equally important, deciding what to do next becomes another task in itself.

What if your task manager showed you less, not more?

You don't have to manage everything at once

A long task list contains different kinds of commitments.

  • Tasks that need attention today.
  • Tasks that are important but can wait.
  • Recurring activities that will come around again.
  • Future commitments that should not be forgotten.
  • Completed tasks that you may want to review later.

These tasks do not all deserve the same amount of attention at the same time.

Instead of constantly reviewing your entire list, you can separate immediate work from everything else.

Meet OnlyThese: A task manager built around attention

OnlyThese is designed around one simple principle: show only what needs attention now.

Its Today section can display a limited number of tasks. You decide how many tasks to keep visible, and you can reorder them as your priorities change.

When you complete a task, another task can enter the visible window. You can bring a different task into focus by reordering your list.

Meanwhile, missed, completed and future tasks remain accessible in their respective sections.

This means you can concentrate on a manageable number of tasks without losing sight of your other commitments.

How it works

1. Set your attention window

Choose how many Today tasks you want displayed at a time.

2. Complete tasks and move forward

As tasks are completed, other tasks can enter your visible Today window.

3. Keep other commitments accessible

Future tasks, missed tasks and completed work remain available without constantly competing for your attention.

Who is OnlyThese for?

OnlyThese may be particularly useful if you:

  • Have multiple interests and ongoing projects.
  • Maintain a long list of personal and professional commitments.
  • Find it difficult to decide what deserves attention today.
  • Want to keep long-term commitments without seeing everything at once.
  • Prefer a simple task manager over a complex productivity system.

Manage your attention, not just your tasks

A task manager should help you act, not create another layer of work.
